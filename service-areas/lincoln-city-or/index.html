From Roads End to Taft

Lincoln City: A Town Built from Five

Lincoln City is not one town that grew outward — it is five towns that merged in 1965. Oceanlake, Delake, Nelscott, Taft, and Cutler City each had their own character, their own building stock, and their own relationship with the coast. That history still shapes the city today. The building styles change noticeably as you drive south along Highway 101, from the newer developments near Roads End to the mid-century beach architecture of Nelscott to the older, more weathered structures of Taft.

What unites all seven miles of Lincoln City is the ocean. Every property here — whether it faces the Pacific directly or sits a few blocks inland — deals with the salt air, fog, wind, and rain that define life on the Central Oregon Coast. And more than perhaps any other city in Lincoln County, the properties here work for a living. Lincoln City has one of the highest concentrations of vacation rentals on the Oregon Coast, which means interiors and exteriors take more wear, need refreshing more often, and often need the work done on a tight schedule.

The Rental Property Challenge

Painting on a Booking Calendar

Lincoln City's vacation rental economy creates a painting challenge you do not find in most towns. Property managers and owners often have only a narrow window between guests to get painting done — sometimes as little as two or three days during shoulder season, and almost no availability during peak summer months. The work has to be planned around the booking calendar, not the other way around.

We Work with Property Managers

We coordinate directly with property managers and rental owners on scheduling. For interior refreshes between guests, we can often complete a standard vacation rental repaint — typically two to three bedrooms, living area, and kitchen — within a compressed timeline if preparation is planned in advance. For larger projects, we recommend targeting the slower months of January through March when longer booking gaps are more available.

The wear pattern on rental properties is different from owner-occupied homes. High-traffic areas like entryways, hallways, and kitchen walls take the heaviest beating. Scuff marks, luggage damage, cleaning product stains, and simple accumulated use from dozens of different families each year all add up. Many rental owners find they need interior touch-ups annually and a full repaint every three to four years to keep their property competitive in the Lincoln City rental market.

Neighborhoods We Serve

Five Towns, Seven Miles, Countless Paint Jobs

Each section of Lincoln City has its own building character and its own maintenance challenges.

Roads End

The northernmost section of Lincoln City sits at the end of the road — literally. Roads End is more exposed to wind and weather than the central neighborhoods, and properties here tend to be a mix of older beach homes and newer vacation builds. The bluff-top homes get hammered by wind-driven salt, and exteriors here may need repainting a year or two sooner than similar properties farther south.

Oceanlake & Delake

These former towns in the northern-central stretch include the Devils Lake area — one of Lincoln City's most distinctive features. Lakeside properties face different moisture challenges than oceanfront homes. Instead of salt spray, they deal with freshwater humidity, morning fog off the lake, and denser vegetation that can trap moisture against siding. The combination creates ideal conditions for mildew growth on exteriors, which means thorough cleaning and mildew-resistant products are essential.

Nelscott & Central Lincoln City

The heart of Lincoln City includes the D River State Wayside, Chinook Winds Casino, and the Lincoln City Outlets. This stretch has the widest variety of property types: single-family beach cottages from the 1960s and 70s, newer multi-unit vacation rentals, commercial storefronts in the outlet district, and the hospitality properties around the casino. Many of the older cottages here have original wood siding that has been painted and repainted many times — proper preparation on these surfaces is critical to avoid peeling.

Taft & Cutler City

The southern end of Lincoln City has a different feel. Taft is the oldest of the five original towns, and its building stock reflects that — smaller homes, tighter lots, some with original cedar siding from the 1940s and 50s. Cutler City, at the southern tip along the Siletz Bay inlet, includes waterfront properties that face tidal exposure rather than open ocean. These neighborhoods tend to be more residential and year-round than the vacation-heavy central sections.

Commercial Painting

Lincoln City's Commercial District

Beyond its residential and vacation rental properties, Lincoln City has a commercial corridor that runs along Highway 101 and into the outlet mall area. Retail storefronts, restaurants, hotels, and motels all need exterior maintenance to stay competitive in a tourism-driven market. First impressions matter when visitors are choosing where to eat, shop, and stay.

We work with Lincoln City business owners on commercial painting projects that can be scheduled during slower periods or completed in phases to minimize disruption to operations. For hotels and motels, we can often paint exterior room sections in sequence so the property never has to close entirely.

The Chinook Winds Casino area and the surrounding commercial properties represent another segment of Lincoln City's commercial painting needs — larger-scale projects that require coordination, proper insurance, and experience with commercial substrates and finishes.
