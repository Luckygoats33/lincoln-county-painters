Painting Multi-Unit Properties: Planning and Coordination

Lincoln County Painters

The Challenge of Multi-Unit Painting

Painting an apartment building, condominium complex, or multi-unit rental property is fundamentally different from painting a single home. You are working around multiple tenants with different schedules, managing access to private spaces, coordinating exterior work that affects everyone, and often dealing with property management companies that need approvals and documentation. On the Oregon Coast, weather adds another layer of complexity.

Planning the Project Scope

Interior Unit Painting

Interior painting of individual units is typically done during tenant turnover. When a tenant moves out, the unit is cleaned, repaired, and painted before the next tenant moves in. This is the most common interior painting scenario for multi-unit properties.

Key considerations for turnover painting:

  • Coordinate timing with cleaning crews, carpet installers, and other contractors to avoid conflicts
  • Use fast-drying, low-VOC paints so the unit is ready quickly
  • Standardize on a few neutral colors to simplify ordering and touch-ups across the property
  • Document the condition before and after painting for your records

Exterior Building Painting

Exterior painting of a multi-unit building is a larger undertaking that affects all tenants simultaneously. Planning is essential.

  • Schedule during lower-occupancy periods when possible. For vacation rental buildings in Lincoln City or Newport, the off-season between November and March may be best.
  • Notify all tenants in advance about the project timeline, including which areas will be affected, when scaffolding will be in place, and any window or balcony restrictions during painting.
  • Plan for parking and access changes that scaffolding or equipment placement may require.

Common Area Painting

Lobbies, hallways, stairwells, and laundry rooms serve all tenants and require painting that minimizes disruption.

  • Paint hallways one section at a time, allowing residents to use alternate routes
  • Schedule stairwell painting during low-traffic hours, ensuring at least one stairway remains accessible
  • Use durable, scuff-resistant paints in high-traffic common areas
  • Post clear signage about wet paint areas and estimated drying times

Color Standards and Consistency

Multi-unit properties benefit from standardized color palettes. This ensures consistency across the property, simplifies future touch-ups, and maintains a cohesive appearance. A defined color standard typically includes:

  • Interior wall color for all units
  • Trim, door, and ceiling colors
  • Common area and hallway colors
  • Exterior body, trim, and accent colors

Having these colors documented by brand and color code means any future touch-up or full repaint matches exactly. Your painting contractor should provide this documentation as part of the project.

Working with Property Managers and HOAs

If you manage the property through a management company or if the building has an HOA, additional considerations apply:

  • Approval processes: HOAs often have color approval committees. Start this process early.
  • Budget approval: Large painting projects may require board approval. Get estimates well in advance of your planned start date.
  • Insurance and licensing: Property managers and HOAs typically require proof of the painting contractor's insurance, bonding, and Oregon CCB license.
  • Warranties: Multi-unit projects should come with clear warranty terms that specify what is covered and for how long.

Coastal Considerations for Multi-Unit Properties

Multi-unit buildings on the Oregon Coast face the same salt air, moisture, and UV challenges as any coastal structure. Oceanfront condos and apartments in Depoe Bay and Waldport may need exterior repainting every five to seven years, compared to eight to ten years for similar inland buildings.
