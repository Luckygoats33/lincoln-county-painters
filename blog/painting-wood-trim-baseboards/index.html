How to Paint Wood Trim and Baseboards Like a Pro

Lincoln County Painters

Why Trim Details Matter

Trim, baseboards, door casings, and crown molding frame every room in your home. When they are painted well, the entire room looks finished and polished. When they are done poorly — with drips, brush marks, or sloppy edges — they become the first thing your eye notices.

Quality trim work is one of the hallmarks of a professional interior painting job, and it is one of the areas where skill and patience make the biggest difference.

Preparation Is Everything

Cleaning and Sanding

Start by cleaning all trim surfaces. Baseboards collect dust, pet hair, and grime along their tops. Door casings accumulate fingerprints and scuff marks. Wash everything with a mild cleaner and let it dry completely.

Sand all trim lightly with 150- or 180-grit sandpaper. This is not about removing old paint — it is about creating a surface that new paint can grip. Pay attention to any areas where old paint is glossy, as fresh paint will not adhere well to a slick surface without scuffing.

Filling and Caulking

Fill any nail holes, dents, or gouges with wood filler. Once dry, sand these spots flush with the surrounding surface. Run a bead of paintable caulk along any gaps where trim meets the wall. This step alone makes trim look dramatically better because it eliminates those shadow lines caused by settling and movement.

Choosing Paint and Finish

Trim paint should be more durable than wall paint because trim surfaces get touched, bumped, and cleaned frequently. Semi-gloss is the traditional choice for baseboards, door casings, and window trim. It wipes clean easily and provides a subtle contrast to the flatter finish on your walls.

Satin is a good alternative if you prefer less sheen. It still offers reasonable durability and washability but with a softer look.

For the smoothest possible finish, consider a high-quality acrylic alkyd hybrid paint. These products level out beautifully and dry to a hard, furniture-like finish without the fumes and long dry times of traditional oil-based paints.

Brush Technique for Smooth Results

Use a high-quality angled brush — two and a half inches is the standard size for most trim work. A good brush holds more paint, lays it down smoothly, and maintains a clean edge.

Load the brush about one-third of the way up the bristles and tap off excess against the inside of the can — do not scrape the brush across the rim, which removes too much paint and creates air bubbles.

Apply paint in long, smooth strokes going with the grain of the wood. On baseboards, work in sections of about three to four feet, brushing from dry into wet to avoid lap marks. Finish each section by lightly running the brush tip along the full length in one continuous stroke — this is called tipping off, and it minimizes brush marks.

Working Order

Paint trim in this order for the cleanest results:

  1. Crown molding (if present)
  2. Door and window casings
  3. Baseboards

If you are painting both walls and trim, paint the trim first and let it dry fully, then cut in the wall color against the trim edges. This approach tends to produce cleaner lines because cutting a flat wall color against a straight trim edge is easier than the reverse.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping sanding between coats: A light scuff between primer and topcoat, and between first and second topcoat, makes each layer adhere better and produces a smoother finish.
  • Using cheap brushes: Bargain brushes leave bristle marks and shed hairs into wet paint. Invest in a quality brush and clean it properly — it will last for years.
  • Rushing dry times: Recoating before paint is fully dry causes dragging, peeling, and an uneven texture. Follow the manufacturer's recoat time recommendations.

Professional Trim Painting

Trim painting is detailed, time-consuming work that rewards patience and experience. For homeowners who want flawless results without spending a weekend on their hands and knees, professional painters bring both the technique and the tools to get trim looking its best.
