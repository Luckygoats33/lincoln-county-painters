Kitchen Painting Tips: Walls, Cabinets, and More

Lincoln County Painters

Painting Your Kitchen: A Room That Works Hard

The kitchen is one of the most used rooms in any home, and that means it needs paint that can handle grease, steam, spills, and constant cleaning. It is also one of the most impactful rooms to repaint because it is a gathering space and a focal point. Whether you are refreshing kitchen walls or considering a full cabinet painting project, here is what you need to know.

Kitchen Walls

Choosing the Right Sheen

Kitchen walls should be painted with a washable, durable finish. Satin or semi-gloss sheens are the best choices for kitchen walls because they resist moisture and wipe clean easily. Flat paint in a kitchen is a mistake. It absorbs grease and stains and is difficult to clean without damaging the finish.

Prep Is Essential

Kitchen walls accumulate more grease and residue than any other room. Before painting, walls must be thoroughly cleaned with a degreasing solution. Painting over greasy surfaces prevents proper paint adhesion and leads to peeling. Pay particular attention to walls near the stove and above countertops.

Color Choices

Kitchens benefit from colors that feel clean and bright. White and off-white remain the most popular kitchen colors because they make the space feel fresh and open. Soft grays, pale blues, and warm greens also work well. Consider how the color relates to your countertops, backsplash, and cabinetry. The goal is a cohesive look that ties the room together.

Kitchen Cabinets

Painting kitchen cabinets is one of the most transformative improvements you can make without a full renovation. It is also one of the most demanding painting projects in terms of skill and preparation.

Why Professional Cabinet Painting Matters

Cabinet painting requires more preparation, more coats, and different techniques than wall painting. Cabinets need to withstand daily handling, moisture, grease, and cleaning. A brush-and-roller job that looks fine on day one may start showing wear within months. Professional painters use a multi-step process:

  1. Remove all doors, drawers, and hardware
  2. Clean every surface with a degreaser
  3. Sand to create a smooth, bondable surface
  4. Apply a bonding primer designed for cabinetry
  5. Apply multiple coats of cabinet-grade paint, typically with a sprayer for a smooth factory finish
  6. Reinstall with new or refreshed hardware

Cabinet Paint and Color

White and off-white remain the most popular cabinet colors, but warm grays, navy, sage green, and even black are gaining popularity. The paint itself should be a cabinet-grade enamel that cures hard and resists chipping, scratching, and yellowing.

Kitchen Trim and Ceiling

Trim

Kitchen trim, including baseboards, window casings, and crown molding, should be painted in a semi-gloss or gloss finish for durability and easy cleaning. White or off-white trim provides a clean frame for whatever wall color you choose.

Ceiling

Kitchen ceilings collect grease and moisture over time. A flat or matte ceiling paint in white or off-white freshens the room and reflects light. Use a paint formulated for ceilings that resists moisture and does not yellow over time.

Timing and Planning

A kitchen painting project temporarily disrupts your cooking routine. Plan ahead:

  • Walls only — Usually completed in one to two days with minimal disruption
  • Cabinets — Takes one to two weeks depending on the kitchen size. Plan for limited use of the kitchen during this time
  • Full kitchen refresh — Walls, cabinets, trim, and ceiling together is the most efficient approach if everything needs attention

Coastal Kitchen Considerations

In Oregon Coast homes, kitchens often deal with higher ambient humidity than inland homes. Using moisture-resistant paints and ensuring good ventilation during and after the project helps the paint cure properly and last longer.
