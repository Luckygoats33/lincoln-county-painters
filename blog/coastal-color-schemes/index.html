Choosing Colors for a Coastal Setting

Selecting paint colors for an Oregon Coast home is different from choosing colors for a home in the suburbs or the city. Your home sits in a landscape of ocean, sky, forest, and fog. The colors you choose should complement this environment, not compete with it. They also need to hold up against the fading, chalking, and weathering that our climate accelerates.

Here are some of the most popular and practical color palettes for homes in Newport, Lincoln City, Depoe Bay, and throughout Lincoln County.

Classic Coastal Neutrals

Neutral color schemes are the most popular choice along the Oregon Coast, and for good reason. They blend with the natural surroundings, look sophisticated in all weather conditions, and age gracefully.

  • Warm whites with slate trim — A clean, timeless look that works with any architectural style
  • Greige (gray-beige) with white trim — Modern and understated, complementing the gray tones of the ocean on overcast days
  • Weathered gray with cream accents — Mimics the natural weathering of coastal wood and feels immediately at home in the landscape
  • Taupe body with white trim and black accents — A three-color scheme that adds depth without being bold

Ocean-Inspired Blues and Greens

Blues and greens connect your home to the ocean and forest landscape that surrounds it. These colors work best in muted, grayed-down versions rather than bright or saturated tones.

  • Slate blue with white trim — Evokes the color of the ocean on overcast days
  • Sage green with cream trim — Blends beautifully with the coastal forest
  • Blue-gray body with navy door — A monochromatic scheme with a strong accent
  • Seafoam body with charcoal trim — Coastal character with modern contrast

Be cautious with greens and blues: some pigments in these hues are more susceptible to UV fading than neutrals. Choose a premium paint with UV-resistant pigments if you go this route.

Natural Wood Tones

Many Oregon Coast homes feature natural wood siding that homeowners prefer to preserve with stains rather than paint. Semi-transparent stains in warm cedar, driftwood gray, or honey tones let the wood grain show through while providing protection.

Deck and fence staining in natural wood tones is especially popular, creating a cohesive look between the house and outdoor living spaces.

Bold Accents Done Right

While neutral bodies dominate coastal home design, accent colors add personality. Popular accent colors for doors, shutters, and architectural details include:

  • Deep red or cranberry — Traditional and welcoming on a front door
  • Navy — Sophisticated and tied to the maritime setting
  • Mustard yellow — A warm pop against gray or blue bodies
  • Black — Modern and dramatic for trim and doors

Keep accent colors to small areas. A bold front door can define your home's character without overwhelming the exterior.

Colors to Think Twice About

Some colors present practical challenges on the coast:

  • Pure white — Shows dirt, mildew, and salt deposits quickly and needs frequent cleaning
  • Very dark colors — Absorb more UV light and heat, fading faster and potentially causing moisture problems in siding
  • Bright reds and yellows — Fade noticeably faster than other hues in UV-heavy environments

Testing Before Committing

Paint colors look different in coastal light than they do under store lighting or on a screen. Always test colors by painting large samples directly on your home. View them at different times of day and in different weather: sunny, overcast, and foggy. A color that looks perfect on a clear afternoon may read very differently under the gray skies that define much of the Oregon Coast year.
