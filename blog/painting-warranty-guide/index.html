Understanding Painting Warranties

Lincoln County Painters

What Your Painting Warranty Actually Covers

When you invest in a professional painting project, a warranty gives you peace of mind that the work will hold up. But painting warranties are often misunderstood. Knowing what is actually covered, what is not, and how the two types of warranties work together helps you evaluate contractors and protect your investment.

Two Types of Warranties

Product Warranty (from the Paint Manufacturer)

Paint manufacturers offer warranties on their products, sometimes called limited lifetime warranties. These warranties typically cover defects in the paint itself, such as premature peeling, blistering, or excessive fading, provided the product was applied according to the manufacturer's specifications.

Important limitations of product warranties:

  • They require the paint to be applied according to the product data sheet (proper surface preparation, temperature and humidity ranges, number of coats, recoat times)
  • They typically cover only the cost of replacement paint, not the labor to apply it
  • They may not cover exterior surfaces in extreme environments (some coastal areas may be excluded or have shorter coverage periods)
  • They require proof of purchase and application details

A product warranty is only as valuable as the manufacturer behind it. Major paint brands with long track records stand behind their warranties more reliably than unknown brands.

Workmanship Warranty (from the Painting Contractor)

The workmanship warranty comes from your painting contractor and covers defects in how the paint was applied. This includes problems like:

  • Peeling or flaking caused by inadequate surface preparation
  • Uneven coverage from improper application
  • Paint failure at joints, corners, or transitions due to poor technique
  • Runs, drips, or other application defects

Workmanship warranties vary by contractor. Some offer one year, others offer two, three, or more years. The duration and terms should be clearly stated in your contract before work begins.

What Warranties Do Not Cover

Understanding exclusions is just as important as understanding coverage. Most painting warranties, whether product or workmanship, do not cover:

  • Normal wear and tear: Fading, dirt accumulation, and gradual weathering over time are expected and not considered defects.
  • Physical damage: Scratches, dents, and impacts from objects, people, or activities are not warranty issues.
  • Moisture intrusion from structural problems: If water is getting behind your siding through a roof leak, failed flashing, or poor drainage, the resulting paint damage is a structural issue, not a paint or application defect.
  • Substrate failure: If the underlying wood rots, plaster crumbles, or drywall is damaged by water, the paint on that surface will fail, but the cause is the substrate, not the paint or its application.
  • Modifications or repairs by others: Work done on or near the painted surface by other contractors can void the warranty for affected areas.

Warranties on the Oregon Coast

Coastal conditions are harder on paint than inland environments. When discussing warranties with a contractor for your coastal exterior project, ask specifically about how they handle warranty claims on ocean-facing surfaces, which experience more aggressive conditions than protected surfaces on the same home.

A realistic contractor will be honest that exterior paint on the coast has a shorter effective life than the same product applied inland. This honesty is preferable to a vague promise of a long warranty that comes with so many exclusions that it is essentially meaningless.

How to Protect Your Warranty

  • Keep documentation. Save your contract, receipts for paint products, and any correspondence with your contractor.
  • Perform basic maintenance. Keep surfaces clean, address drainage issues promptly, and trim vegetation away from painted surfaces.
  • Report issues early. If you notice a problem, contact your contractor sooner rather than later. Small issues are easier and less expensive to address than problems that have been left to worsen.
  • Do not modify the work. Avoid having other contractors work on or near the painted surfaces without notifying your painting contractor, as this may affect warranty coverage.

Evaluating Warranty Offers

When comparing contractors, look beyond the warranty duration. A three-year warranty from a contractor with a strong reputation, clear terms, and a track record of honoring claims is more valuable than a ten-year warranty from a contractor who may not be in business in ten years or whose warranty terms are so restrictive that no claim would ever qualify.

Ask contractors to explain their warranty terms clearly and put everything in writing. If the terms are not in your contract, they do not exist.
