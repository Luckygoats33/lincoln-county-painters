Common Painting Mistakes Homeowners Make

Lincoln County Painters

Learn from Others' Painting Mistakes

Whether you are planning a DIY painting project or hiring a contractor, understanding common painting mistakes helps you avoid them. These are the errors we see most often in Lincoln County, along with what to do instead.

Skipping Surface Preparation

This is the single most common mistake in painting, and it is the one with the most serious consequences. Painting over dirty, greasy, glossy, or loose surfaces causes the new paint to fail, sometimes within months. On the Oregon Coast, where moisture and mildew are constant factors, skipping prep is even more damaging.

What to do instead: Clean every surface before painting. Remove loose paint. Sand glossy surfaces. Fill holes and cracks. Prime bare surfaces. This is where most of the work in a paint job happens, and it is the work that determines how long your paint lasts.

Choosing the Wrong Paint for the Application

Not all paint is the same, and using the wrong product for the application leads to problems. Common mismatches include:

  • Using interior paint on exterior surfaces (it lacks weather resistance)
  • Using flat paint in kitchens and bathrooms (it cannot be scrubbed clean)
  • Using standard paint on cabinets (it lacks the hardness and adhesion needed for surfaces that get daily use)
  • Using budget paint on coastal exteriors (it does not have the resin content needed for salt and UV resistance)

What to do instead: Match the paint product to the specific application. Ask your paint supplier or contractor about the right product for each surface and environment.

Not Priming When Needed

Primer is not optional in many situations. New drywall, bare wood, repaired areas, stained surfaces, and transitions between different paint types all need primer. Skipping it leads to uneven coverage, stain bleed-through, and adhesion failure.

What to do instead: Use the appropriate primer for each situation. PVA primer for new drywall, stain-blocking primer for stained or tannin-rich wood, bonding primer for glossy surfaces, and rust-inhibiting primer for metal.

Buying the Cheapest Paint Available

Budget paint costs less per gallon but often requires more coats for adequate coverage, fades faster, does not clean as easily, and fails sooner than quality products. Over the life of the paint job, cheap paint almost always costs more because you repaint sooner.

What to do instead: Buy the best paint you can afford. The difference in material cost between budget and premium paint is a small fraction of the total project cost, which is dominated by labor. Quality paint makes that labor last longer.

Applying Paint Too Thick

More paint in a single coat is not better. Thick coats sag, drip, dry unevenly, and take longer to cure. They may look fine initially but are more prone to cracking and peeling over time because the film does not cure properly all the way through.

What to do instead: Apply two thin, even coats rather than one thick one. Each coat should be smooth and uniform, with proper drying time between them.

Painting in Bad Conditions

Temperature, humidity, and wind all affect how paint applies and cures. Painting when it is too cold, too hot, too humid, or too windy leads to a range of problems including poor adhesion, blistering, slow curing, and uneven finish.

What to do instead: Follow the paint manufacturer's application guidelines for temperature and humidity. On the Oregon Coast, this often means painting exteriors during the drier summer months and monitoring conditions carefully.

Ignoring the Second Coat

One coat rarely provides adequate coverage, even with premium paint. A single coat may look acceptable when wet but reveals thin spots, uneven coverage, and the old color showing through once it dries. Two coats provide the color depth, uniformity, and durability that a quality paint job requires.

What to do instead: Always plan for two topcoats. Allow proper drying time between coats as specified by the manufacturer.

Poor Cutting-In and Edging

The transition between wall color and trim, ceiling, or adjacent walls is where amateur work is most visible. Wavy lines, paint bleed, and uneven edges detract from the overall appearance of the paint job.

What to do instead: Use quality painter's tape for crisp lines. If cutting in freehand, use a quality angled brush and take your time. This is a skill that improves with practice, and it is one of the main reasons professional painters deliver better results.

Not Maintaining Painted Surfaces

Even the best paint job needs maintenance. Ignoring small problems like peeling at a corner, a crack in the caulking, or mildew starting to grow allows those issues to expand into much larger and more expensive problems.

What to do instead: Inspect painted surfaces at least annually. Address small issues promptly before they grow. On the coast, this is especially important for exterior surfaces exposed to the elements.
