Can You Paint Over Wallpaper? What to Know

Lincoln County Painters

The Short Answer

Can you paint over wallpaper? Technically, yes. Should you? It depends. Painting over wallpaper can be a practical solution in some situations, but it can also create problems that are difficult and expensive to fix. Before you decide, consider the factors below.

When Painting Over Wallpaper Can Work

Painting over wallpaper is sometimes the right choice when:

  • The wallpaper is firmly adhered — If the wallpaper is solidly bonded to the wall with no peeling edges, bubbles, or loose seams, painting over it may produce an acceptable result.
  • The wallpaper is smooth — Flat, non-textured wallpaper is a better candidate for painting. Textured or embossed wallpaper will show its pattern through the paint.
  • Removing it would cause more damage — In some cases, especially in older homes, the wallpaper has been up for decades and removing it would take the drywall surface with it. Painting over it may be the less destructive option.
  • It is a temporary solution — If you are preparing a home for sale or need a quick refresh, painting over wallpaper can be a practical short-term fix.

When You Should Remove the Wallpaper

Removing the wallpaper before painting is the better approach when:

  • The wallpaper is peeling — If edges are lifting or sections are coming loose, the wallpaper will continue to separate under the paint, and the paint will peel with it.
  • Multiple layers are present — Older homes in Newport, Toledo, and across Lincoln County sometimes have two or more layers of wallpaper stacked on top of each other. Painting over multiple layers creates an unstable surface.
  • The wallpaper has heavy texture — Embossed patterns, grasscloth, or vinyl wallpaper with texture will telegraph through paint and look like painted wallpaper, not painted walls.
  • You want a high-quality, long-lasting finish — For the best possible result, removing wallpaper and painting the clean wall surface is always superior.
  • There is moisture damage — In the humid Oregon Coast climate, moisture behind wallpaper can cause mold and adhesion problems. Removing the wallpaper lets you assess and address the wall surface.

How to Paint Over Wallpaper Properly

If you decide that painting over wallpaper is the right choice for your situation, follow these steps:

Step 1: Repair the Surface

Glue down any loose edges or seams with wallpaper seam adhesive. Fill any tears or damage with a thin layer of spackle and sand smooth.

Step 2: Clean the Surface

Wash the wallpaper to remove dust, grease, and grime. Let it dry completely.

Step 3: Apply Primer

This is the most important step. Apply a shellac-based or oil-based primer over the entire surface. These primers seal the wallpaper, prevent the adhesive from reactivating when wet paint is applied, and prevent patterns or colors from bleeding through. Do not skip this step. Latex primer alone may cause the wallpaper adhesive to loosen.

Step 4: Skim Coat Seams (Optional)

If wallpaper seams are visible, you can apply a thin skim coat of joint compound over them and sand smooth. This helps minimize the appearance of seams through the final paint.

Step 5: Apply Paint

Once the primer is fully dry, apply two coats of your chosen interior paint. Use a good quality roller to maintain even coverage.

Wallpaper Removal: The Better Option

If you do opt for removal, the process involves:

  1. Scoring the wallpaper surface to allow moisture to penetrate
  2. Applying a wallpaper removal solution or steamer
  3. Scraping the wallpaper and adhesive residue
  4. Cleaning and repairing the wall surface
  5. Priming and painting

Removal is more work upfront but produces a far superior finished result. The walls will look like properly painted walls, not like painted-over wallpaper.

Let a Professional Help You Decide

A professional painter can assess your wallpaper's condition and advise whether painting over it or removing it is the better choice for your specific situation.
