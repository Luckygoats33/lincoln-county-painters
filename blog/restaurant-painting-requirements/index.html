Restaurant Painting: Health Code and Appearance Considerations

Lincoln County Painters

Why Restaurant Painting Requires Special Attention

Painting a restaurant is not like painting any other commercial space. You are working around food preparation areas, dining spaces where customers make judgments about cleanliness, and a business that often cannot close for extended periods. Oregon health codes, customer expectations, and the practical demands of a restaurant environment all shape how the painting project should be planned and executed.

Health Code Considerations

Oregon health inspectors pay attention to the condition of walls, ceilings, and surfaces in food preparation and serving areas. While specific paint requirements may vary by jurisdiction, several principles apply to restaurants across Lincoln County.

Washable Surfaces in Food Areas

Walls and ceilings in kitchens and food preparation areas should be painted with finishes that can be washed and sanitized. Semi-gloss and high-gloss paints are preferred in these areas because they resist moisture, wipe clean easily, and do not harbor bacteria as readily as flat finishes.

Peeling and Flaking

Peeling or flaking paint in any restaurant area is a health concern. Paint chips can contaminate food and signal poor maintenance to inspectors. If your restaurant has any areas where paint is failing, addressing it should be a priority.

Painting During Food Service

Never paint in or near food preparation areas while food is being prepared or served. Paint fumes, dust from sanding, and the risk of paint drips all create food safety hazards. All painting in kitchen areas should happen during closures or well outside of operating hours.

Choosing the Right Paint for Restaurant Spaces

Dining Areas

Your dining room is where customers form their impression of your restaurant. Colors should complement your brand and create the atmosphere you want. Warmer tones tend to feel inviting and comfortable, while cooler colors can create a more modern or sophisticated feel.

Use eggshell or satin finishes in dining areas. They provide a subtle sheen that looks polished, cleans up well, and does not show scuffs as readily as flat paint.

Kitchen and Prep Areas

Semi-gloss or high-gloss finishes are the standard for kitchen walls and ceilings. They are moisture-resistant, easy to clean, and withstand the heat and grease that are part of every commercial kitchen. Light colors make it easier to spot areas that need cleaning and improve visibility for staff.

Restrooms

Restrooms should be painted with mildew-resistant, semi-gloss paint. These spaces see heavy moisture and need frequent cleaning, so durability matters. Customers often judge a restaurant by its restrooms, so keeping them freshly painted is worth the investment.

Scheduling Restaurant Painting

Most restaurant painting happens in phases designed around the business schedule.

  • Closures and holidays: If your restaurant closes one day per week, that day can be used for painting. Major holidays or planned closures provide larger windows.
  • Off-season: Coastal restaurants in Newport and Lincoln City often see slower winter months, making this an ideal time for larger projects.
  • Overnight work: Some painting can be done after closing and completed before the next day's opening, though this depends on the scope and ventilation requirements.

Ventilation and Drying

Low-VOC and zero-VOC paints are strongly recommended for restaurant environments. Even these products require ventilation during and after application. Plan for adequate drying time before food preparation resumes in any painted area. In enclosed spaces without windows, fans and open doors help circulate fresh air.

First Impressions Start at the Door

Your restaurant's exterior creates the first impression. A well-maintained facade with fresh paint communicates quality and care, traits customers transfer to their expectations of the food. A faded, peeling exterior sends the opposite message.
