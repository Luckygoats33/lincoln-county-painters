Cheap vs. Quality Paint: The Real Cost Difference

Lincoln County Painters

Why Paint Quality Matters More Than Paint Price

When you look at the price per gallon, the difference between budget and premium paint can feel significant. A gallon of builder-grade paint might cost half as much as a premium product. But paint price per gallon is misleading because it ignores everything else that determines the real cost of your painting project: coverage, durability, appearance, and longevity.

What Makes Quality Paint Different

Higher Pigment Content

Premium paints contain more pigment, the solid particles that provide color and opacity. Higher pigment content means better coverage per coat. You may be able to achieve full coverage in two coats with a premium paint where a budget paint requires three. Since labor is the majority of any painting project's cost, needing fewer coats saves money.

Better Binders

Binders are the resins that hold the pigment together and make the paint adhere to the surface. Quality paints use superior binders that create a more durable, flexible film. This matters greatly on the Oregon Coast, where wood and siding expand and contract with moisture changes. A more flexible paint film resists cracking and peeling as the substrate moves.

Improved Additives

Premium paints include additives for specific performance characteristics: UV stabilizers that resist fading, mildewcides that prevent mold growth, and flow agents that improve application. For coastal homes, UV and mildew resistance are particularly valuable.

Lower Filler Content

Budget paints bulk up their volume with inexpensive fillers like calcium carbonate. These fillers add volume but not performance. They dilute the paint's protective qualities and contribute to a chalky appearance as the paint weathers.

Real-World Performance Differences

Coverage

A premium exterior paint might cover 350 to 400 square feet per gallon with good hiding, while a budget product covers 250 to 300 square feet per gallon and may still show the old color underneath. Fewer gallons needed and fewer coats applied mean less material cost and less labor cost, narrowing the price gap considerably.

Durability

On the Central Oregon Coast, a quality exterior paint can last seven to ten years before needing repainting. A budget exterior paint may start showing wear in three to five years. If you repaint with cheap paint twice in the time it takes to repaint once with quality paint, you are spending more on labor each cycle, more on materials total, and enduring more disruption to your life.

Appearance

Premium paints look better. They flow more smoothly, level more evenly, and maintain their color more consistently. The finish quality is visible in the final result, particularly in interior applications where you see the walls up close every day.

The True Cost Calculation

Consider an exterior painting project where labor costs are roughly the same regardless of paint quality (because the amount of prep and painting time is similar):

  • Budget paint that lasts five years before needing replacement means paying for a full painting project every five years
  • Premium paint that lasts ten years means paying for one project in the same timeframe
  • The material cost difference between budget and premium paint is a fraction of the total project cost, which is dominated by labor

Over twenty years, the homeowner using quality paint spends less total money and deals with fewer painting projects.

When Budget Paint Makes Sense

There are limited situations where budget paint is appropriate:

  • Rental unit turnovers where the paint will be covered again within a couple of years
  • Temporary spaces or structures with a short expected lifespan
  • Primer coats (though even here, quality primer improves the final result)

Our Recommendation

For homes on the Oregon Coast, we consistently recommend quality paints formulated for coastal conditions. The additional material cost is modest relative to the total project, and the performance difference is substantial. When you invest in professional exterior or interior painting, the paint itself should not be where you cut corners.
