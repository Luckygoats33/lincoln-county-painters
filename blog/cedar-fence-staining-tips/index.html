Cedar Fence Staining Tips for Oregon Weather

Lincoln County Painters

Why Cedar Fences Need Staining on the Oregon Coast

Cedar is a popular fence material in Lincoln County because it naturally resists rot and insects. However, without protection, even cedar will gray, crack, and deteriorate when exposed to the relentless moisture, UV light, and salt air along the Central Oregon Coast. Staining preserves the wood, extends its lifespan, and keeps your fence looking the way you intended.

Choosing the Right Time to Stain

Timing is critical for fence staining on the coast. The wood needs to be dry, and you need a window of dry weather for the stain to cure properly.

  • Best months: July through mid-September typically offer the most reliable dry stretches in Lincoln County.
  • Temperature: Most stains require temperatures between 50 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit. Coastal Oregon rarely gets too hot, but mornings can be cool, so starting mid-morning is often ideal.
  • New cedar: Wait at least two to four weeks after installation before staining new cedar. The wood needs time to dry and open its pores to accept the stain.

Preparing Your Cedar Fence for Stain

Preparation determines how well the stain adheres and how long it lasts. Skip this step and you will be restaining far sooner than necessary.

Cleaning

Use a wood cleaner or brightener to remove dirt, mildew, and the gray oxidation layer. A pump sprayer works well for applying cleaner. Let it sit according to the product directions, then rinse with a garden hose or a pressure washer on a low setting. Cedar is soft, so keep the pressure washer nozzle at least 12 inches from the surface and use a fan tip, not a pinpoint tip.

Sanding

If the surface feels rough or fuzzy after cleaning, light sanding with 80 to 100 grit sandpaper smooths the grain and helps the stain absorb evenly. This step is especially important if you are restaining over an old finish.

Drying

After cleaning, allow the fence to dry completely. This usually takes 48 to 72 hours in coastal conditions. The wood should feel dry to the touch and not show any dark, damp areas.

Selecting Your Stain

For cedar fences in coastal Oregon, consider these options:

  • Semi-transparent stain — The most popular choice for cedar. It lets the natural grain show through while adding color and UV protection.
  • Transparent stain — Shows the most wood grain but offers the least UV protection. You will need to reapply more frequently.
  • Solid stain — Hides the grain completely but provides maximum UV and moisture protection. Good for older cedar with cosmetic imperfections.

Look for stains with UV inhibitors and mildewcides. On the coast, mildew growth is a constant concern, so built-in mildew resistance is worth prioritizing.

Application Tips

  • Stir the stain thoroughly before and during application. Pigments settle to the bottom.
  • Apply with a brush, roller, or sprayer. Brushing works best for working the stain into the wood grain, but spraying followed by back-brushing is the fastest approach for long fence runs.
  • Work in manageable sections, staining from top to bottom to catch drips.
  • Apply two thin coats rather than one thick coat. Thick application leads to sticky surfaces and uneven drying.
  • Stain both sides of the fence if accessible. This provides even moisture protection and prevents warping.

Maintenance Schedule

On the Central Oregon Coast, plan to inspect your cedar fence annually. Semi-transparent stains typically last two to three years on fences. When water no longer beads on the surface, it is time to restain. Transparent stains may need attention every one to two years.
