Exterior Painting Preparation: The Steps That Matter Most

Lincoln County Painters

Why Preparation Determines How Long Your Paint Lasts

The quality of an exterior paint job is determined long before any color goes on the house. Preparation — cleaning, scraping, sanding, priming, caulking, and protecting — accounts for the majority of labor on a well-done exterior project. It is also the reason professional results last years longer than shortcuts.

On the Oregon Coast, where homes face salt air, driving rain, and persistent moisture, cutting corners on preparation means your paint will fail sooner. The surface beneath the paint matters more than the paint itself.

Step 1: Inspection

Before any work begins, a thorough inspection identifies every issue that needs to be addressed. Walk the entire exterior and look for peeling or flaking paint, cracked caulk, rotted wood, mildew growth, chalking surfaces, and areas where moisture may be getting behind the siding.

Each of these conditions requires a different treatment. Lumping them all together under a quick power wash and paint leads to problems within a year or two.

Step 2: Cleaning

Every exterior surface needs to be clean before painting. On the coast, this means removing not just dirt but salt deposits, mildew, moss, and algae. Pressure washing is the standard approach, but it must be done carefully — too much pressure can damage wood siding, force water behind trim boards, and gouge soft surfaces.

For mildew-affected areas, cleaning with a mildew-killing solution before or during washing ensures the mildew is eliminated rather than just rinsed away. Painting over mildew means it will grow back through the new paint.

Step 3: Scraping and Sanding

Any loose, peeling, or flaking paint must be removed. Scraping takes it down to a stable edge, and sanding feathers those edges smooth so the transition between old paint and bare wood is not visible under the new coat.

On homes with multiple layers of old paint — common in older neighborhoods in Newport and Toledo — this can be the most time-consuming step of the entire project. But it cannot be skipped. Paint applied over loose old paint will peel right along with it.

Step 4: Repairs

Damaged wood, rotted trim, cracked siding, and deteriorated caulk all need to be repaired before painting. Small areas of rot can be stabilized with wood hardener and filled with epoxy filler. Larger sections may need replacement boards.

All joints, gaps around windows and doors, and seams between siding courses should be caulked with a high-quality, paintable exterior caulk. This prevents moisture from getting behind the paint and causing it to fail prematurely.

Step 5: Priming

Bare wood, repaired areas, and stain-prone surfaces need primer before topcoat. Primer provides adhesion, seals porous surfaces, and blocks tannin bleed from cedar and other woods common in Oregon construction. Skipping primer on bare wood is one of the most common reasons exterior paint fails early. Learn more about why primer matters.

Step 6: Protection

Before painting begins, all surfaces that should not be painted need to be masked and protected. This includes windows, doors, hardware, light fixtures, deck surfaces, landscaping, and concrete. Proper masking takes time but prevents costly cleanup and damage.

What Happens When Prep Is Skipped

Homes where preparation is rushed or skipped show problems quickly: peeling within the first year, visible mildew returning through the paint, caulk lines cracking and separating, and paint that chalks and fades faster than it should.

Lincoln County Painters builds thorough preparation into every exterior painting project. It is not an add-on or an upcharge — it is simply how exterior painting should be done, especially in our coastal climate.
