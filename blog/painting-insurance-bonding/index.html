Protecting Yourself When Hiring a Painter

Hiring a painter to work on your home involves trust. You are inviting someone onto your property to work on one of your largest investments. Insurance, bonding, and proper licensing exist to protect you if something goes wrong. Understanding what these protections mean helps you hire with confidence.

Oregon CCB Licensing

In Oregon, anyone performing construction work, including painting, for compensation is required to hold an active license with the Oregon Construction Contractors Board (CCB). This is not optional; it is state law.

What a CCB License Means

  • The contractor has met Oregon's requirements for business registration
  • They carry the minimum required insurance and bonding
  • They are subject to the CCB's complaint resolution process if disputes arise
  • Their license status and any complaints are publicly searchable on the CCB website

Why It Matters to You

If you hire an unlicensed contractor and something goes wrong, you have limited recourse. The CCB cannot help resolve disputes with unlicensed contractors. Additionally, if an unlicensed worker is injured on your property, your homeowner's insurance may not cover the liability, potentially leaving you personally responsible.

General Liability Insurance

General liability insurance protects you, the homeowner, if the painting contractor causes damage to your property during the project. Examples include:

  • A ladder scratches or dents your siding
  • Paint is spilled on your driveway, landscaping, or flooring
  • A pressure washer damages a window seal
  • Scaffolding damages your roof or gutters

Without liability insurance, the homeowner would have to pursue the contractor personally for damages, which is costly, time-consuming, and uncertain. With proper insurance, the contractor's policy covers the damage.

Workers' Compensation Insurance

Workers' compensation insurance covers the painting crew if someone is injured on the job. Painting involves ladders, scaffolding, heights, chemicals, and physical labor. Injuries can and do happen.

If a painter without workers' compensation insurance is injured on your property, you could be held liable for their medical bills and lost wages. Oregon law requires employers with one or more employees to carry workers' compensation insurance. A solo contractor may be exempt but should still carry coverage for your protection and theirs.

Bonding

A surety bond is a financial guarantee that the contractor will fulfill their obligations. If the contractor fails to complete the work, performs it deficiently, or violates the terms of the contract, the bond provides a mechanism for you to recover some of your investment.

In Oregon, the CCB requires a minimum bond amount for all licensed contractors. Some contractors carry bonds that exceed the minimum, which provides greater protection for the homeowner.

How to Verify a Contractor's Coverage

Before hiring a painter, take these steps:

  • Ask for their CCB license number and verify it on the Oregon CCB website (ccb.oregon.gov). Check that the license is active and the correct type for painting work.
  • Request a certificate of insurance showing general liability and workers' compensation coverage. A reputable contractor will provide this without hesitation.
  • Check for complaints on the CCB website. While complaints do not necessarily mean a contractor is bad, a pattern of unresolved complaints is a red flag.

The Cost of Cutting Corners

Unlicensed, uninsured painters often offer lower prices. The savings can be tempting. But the financial risk you assume by hiring an unprotected contractor can far exceed any savings. A single accident or property damage incident could cost you thousands of dollars, none of which would be covered.

Licensed, insured, and bonded contractors charge enough to cover their insurance premiums and business costs. This is built into their pricing because it is part of operating a legitimate, responsible business.
