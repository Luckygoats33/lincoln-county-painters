How Long Does an Exterior Paint Job Take?

Lincoln County Painters

The Short Answer

A typical exterior paint job on a single-family home takes three to seven working days. A small home in good condition with minimal prep work might be completed in two to three days. A larger home with significant preparation needs, multiple colors, or complex architectural details could take a week or more.

On the Oregon Coast, weather can extend these timelines. Rain delays, fog that keeps surfaces damp into mid-morning, and the need to stop work when dew starts forming in the evening all reduce productive painting hours.

What Determines the Timeline

Home Size

A 1,200-square-foot home has roughly half the exterior surface area of a 2,400-square-foot home. Larger homes simply take longer to prepare, prime, and paint. Multi-story homes also require more setup time for ladders, scaffolding, or lifts.

Surface Condition

This is often the biggest variable. A home with paint in reasonable condition — no major peeling, minimal repairs needed — requires less preparation time. A home with extensive peeling, failed caulk, rotted trim, and heavy mildew growth may need as much time for preparation as for actual painting.

On coastal homes, thorough preparation is especially important because the coastal environment demands it. Cutting corners on prep to save time always costs more in the long run.

Number of Colors

A single body color with matching trim is straightforward. Multiple body colors, contrasting trim, different accent colors on doors and shutters, and decorative details each add time for cutting in, masking, and careful application.

Architectural Complexity

Dormers, gables, bay windows, decorative brackets, and multi-level rooflines all add time compared to a simple rectangular structure. These features require more ladder moves, more cutting in, and more careful work.

Weather Conditions

On the Lincoln County coast, weather is a constant factor in scheduling. Rain stops work entirely. Fog and heavy dew can delay starts until surfaces dry in mid-morning. High winds make ladder work unsafe. Experienced coastal painters build weather contingency into their schedules.

A Typical Timeline Breakdown

For a medium-sized home (around 2,000 square feet) in fair condition, here is a rough breakdown of how time is spent:

  • Day 1: Setup, pressure washing, and initial scraping
  • Day 2: Continued scraping, sanding, caulking, and repairs
  • Day 3: Priming and spot-priming
  • Day 4: First coat of body color
  • Day 5: Second coat of body color, begin trim
  • Day 6: Finish trim, detail work, cleanup, and walkthrough

This timeline assumes a crew of two to three painters and cooperative weather.

Factors That Can Extend the Timeline

  • Extensive wood rot requiring carpentry repairs before painting
  • Multiple layers of old paint that need removal
  • Lead paint requiring lead-safe containment and procedures
  • Difficult access requiring scaffolding setup
  • Weather delays
