When Should You Restain Your Deck?

Lincoln County Painters

How to Know Your Deck Needs Attention

Deck stain does not last forever. It breaks down gradually from UV exposure, foot traffic, moisture, and weather. On the Oregon Coast, where decks face salt air, rain, fog, and humidity year-round, stain wears faster than in drier inland climates. Knowing when to restain — before the wood is damaged — saves you money and extends the life of your deck.

Simple Tests to Check Your Stain

The Water Test

Sprinkle water on several areas of your deck — especially high-traffic zones and areas with the most sun or weather exposure. If the water beads up on the surface, the stain is still providing moisture protection. If the water soaks into the wood within a few seconds and creates a dark wet spot, the stain has worn through and the wood is absorbing moisture. Time to restain.

Visual Inspection

Look for these signs that your stain is losing its effectiveness:

  • Graying: Wood turning gray or silver means UV protection has failed and the wood is weathering
  • Fading: The original stain color has significantly lightened or become uneven
  • Bare spots: Areas where the stain has worn away completely, exposing raw wood
  • Peeling or flaking: Solid stain or film-forming products may peel as they age
  • Mildew growth: Dark spots or green growth indicate moisture is penetrating the wood
  • Splintering: Raised fibers and splinters mean the wood surface is deteriorating

Typical Restaining Intervals

How often you need to restain depends on several factors:

By Product Type

  • Clear sealers: Every 1 to 2 years
  • Transparent stains: Every 1 to 2 years
  • Semi-transparent stains: Every 2 to 3 years
  • Solid stains: Every 3 to 5 years

These are general guidelines. On the Oregon Coast, expect the shorter end of each range due to the demanding climate.

By Exposure

Decks with southern or western exposure receive more UV and weather, wearing stain faster. A protected, covered deck lasts longer between applications than an open, exposed deck. Ocean-facing decks in Depoe Bay or Yachats may need attention annually if fully exposed to salt spray and prevailing winds.

By Traffic

Areas where people walk regularly — the path from the door to the stairs, around the grill area, the top of the stairs — wear through stain faster than low-traffic edges and railings.

The Cost of Waiting Too Long

Waiting until the wood is visibly damaged costs more than timely restaining. When stain fails completely and wood is left unprotected:

  • The wood surface degrades, requiring more aggressive sanding to restore
  • Moisture penetration causes checking, splitting, and eventually rot
  • Gray, weathered wood requires more extensive cleaning and preparation
  • Severely damaged boards may need replacement before restaining is possible

Maintaining a regular staining schedule is significantly less expensive than restoring a neglected deck.

Best Time to Restain on the Coast

The ideal window for deck staining on the Oregon Coast is late June through September, when you are most likely to get the dry weather needed for proper stain application and curing. The deck surface should be dry, and the forecast should show at least 24 to 48 hours without rain after application.

Spring and early fall can work if weather cooperates, but summer remains the safest bet. For more seasonal planning, see our guide to coastal Oregon deck maintenance.
