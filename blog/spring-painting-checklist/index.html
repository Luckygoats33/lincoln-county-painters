Spring Painting Checklist for Oregon Homeowners

Lincoln County Painters

Spring Is Assessment Season

After months of Oregon Coast rain, wind, and occasional storms, spring is the time to evaluate how your home's paint held up through winter. The goal is not to rush into painting — spring can still be too wet for exterior work — but to assess what needs attention, plan your projects, and get on a painter's schedule before the busy summer season fills up.

Exterior Inspection Checklist

Walk the entire perimeter of your home and look closely at every surface. Bring a notepad or use your phone to document what you find.

Paint Condition

  • Peeling or flaking: Any areas where paint has lifted from the surface need attention before moisture causes further damage.
  • Cracking: Fine surface cracks (crazing) indicate paint is becoming brittle. Larger cracks can allow water to reach the substrate.
  • Fading: Compare sun-exposed surfaces to sheltered areas. Significant fading means the paint's UV protection is diminishing.
  • Chalking: Rub your hand across the paint. If it leaves a powdery residue, the paint is chalking and breaking down.

Surface Condition

  • Mildew or mold: Dark spots, green patches, or fuzzy growth indicate moisture problems. These need treatment before painting.
  • Wood rot: Press on window sills, door frames, trim boards, and siding edges. Soft or spongy wood needs repair or replacement.
  • Caulk failure: Check caulking around windows, doors, and trim joints. Cracked or missing caulk allows water intrusion.
  • Rust stains: Nail heads, metal flashing, or hardware that is rusting and staining the surrounding paint.

Interior Assessment

Spring is also a good time to evaluate interior surfaces:

  • Look for water stains on ceilings and walls near windows — these may indicate roof or window leaks that appeared during winter rains
  • Check bathrooms for mildew growth on painted surfaces
  • Note any rooms where paint has worn, scuffed, or yellowed over the year
  • Assess trim and baseboards for chips and wear

Planning Your Spring and Summer Projects

Once you have a clear picture of what needs attention, prioritize your painting projects:

High Priority

  • Exterior areas with peeling paint or exposed wood (these are actively deteriorating)
  • Water-damaged interior surfaces (fix the leak first, then repaint)
  • Rotted wood that needs replacement and painting

Medium Priority

  • Exterior surfaces with significant fading or chalking
  • Rooms that need refreshing for comfort or resale preparation
  • Deck and fence staining that is overdue

Lower Priority

  • Cosmetic updates for rooms in good condition
  • Color changes that are purely aesthetic preferences

Scheduling Ahead

Professional painters in Lincoln County book up during the summer painting season. If your inspection reveals exterior work that needs to happen, spring is the time to schedule. Many painting contractors can begin exterior work in late May or June when weather permits, but their calendars fill quickly.

Interior painting can be done any time of year, so if you want interior work done during the drier months while the painter is already at your home, discuss this during the estimate.
