Why Pressure Washing Before Painting Is Essential

Lincoln County Painters

Paint Does Not Stick to Dirty Surfaces

It sounds obvious, but it is the most important reason to pressure wash before painting: paint needs a clean surface to bond properly. Dirt, dust, pollen, mildew, algae, cobwebs, and salt deposits all create a barrier between the paint and the surface underneath. When paint is applied over contamination, it bonds to the dirt rather than the siding — and when that dirt eventually loosens, the paint comes off with it.

On the Oregon Coast, this issue is amplified. Salt spray from the ocean, persistent moisture, and mild temperatures that encourage biological growth mean exterior surfaces accumulate contamination faster than homes in drier climates.

What Pressure Washing Removes

  • Dirt and grime: General buildup from weather, traffic, and the environment
  • Mildew and algae: Extremely common on north-facing walls and shaded areas in coastal Oregon
  • Salt deposits: Invisible but present on homes near the ocean, salt crystals interfere with paint adhesion
  • Chalking paint: As paint ages, it develops a powdery surface called chalk. This must be removed before new paint is applied
  • Loose paint: Pressure washing helps identify and remove paint that is already failing
  • Cobwebs and insect residue: Particularly around eaves, soffits, and window frames

Proper Pressure Washing Technique

Pressure washing for paint preparation is not the same as pressure washing a driveway. Siding, especially wood siding, requires careful technique to avoid damage.

Pressure Settings

Wood siding should be washed at lower pressure — typically 1,200 to 1,500 PSI — to avoid gouging the wood grain or forcing water behind boards. Fiber cement and vinyl can handle slightly higher pressure but still benefit from moderation.

Nozzle and Distance

Use a wide-angle nozzle (25 or 40 degrees) rather than a zero-degree tip, which concentrates water into a damaging jet. Keep the nozzle at least 12 inches from the surface and work at a consistent distance.

Direction

Always wash from top to bottom and angle the spray downward. Spraying upward under lap siding forces water behind the boards, which can cause interior damage and creates moisture problems that lead to paint failure.

Adding a Cleaning Solution

For mildew and algae, plain water is not enough. A mildew-killing cleaning solution should be applied before or during pressure washing. This kills the organisms at the root level. Without a biocide, mildew spores remain in the wood grain and regrow through the new paint — sometimes within months.

In communities like Depoe Bay and Yachats, where ocean spray and shade create ideal conditions for mildew, this step is especially critical.

Drying Time After Washing

After pressure washing, surfaces need time to dry completely before painting. Wood siding typically needs 24 to 48 hours of dry weather before it is ready for primer or paint. Painting on damp wood traps moisture beneath the paint film, leading to peeling and blistering.

On the coast, where humidity is often high and drying is slower, professional painters plan their schedule around washing and drying windows to ensure surfaces are properly prepared.

Pressure Washing as Part of Complete Preparation

Pressure washing is one step in a thorough exterior painting preparation process. It is followed by scraping, sanding, repairs, caulking, and priming before any topcoat is applied. Each step builds on the previous one, and skipping any of them compromises the final result.
