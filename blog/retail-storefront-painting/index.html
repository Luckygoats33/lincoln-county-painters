Retail Storefront Painting: First Impressions Matter

Lincoln County Painters

Your Storefront Is Your Biggest Advertisement

Before a customer walks through your door, they have already formed an opinion about your business based on how it looks from the outside. A well-maintained, freshly painted storefront communicates quality, attention to detail, and professionalism. A faded or peeling exterior suggests the opposite, regardless of how good your products or services actually are.

This is especially true in tourist-driven communities on the Central Oregon Coast. Visitors walking through Newport's Nye Beach, the shops along Highway 101 in Lincoln City, or the downtown storefronts in Depoe Bay make quick decisions about which shops to enter. Your exterior paint is part of that split-second judgment.

Color and Branding

Your storefront color should align with your brand identity while also fitting harmoniously within the streetscape. Consider these factors when choosing colors:

  • Brand consistency: Your exterior colors should reflect your brand. If you have established brand colors, use them on your signage, trim, or accent elements.
  • Visibility: Your storefront should be easy to spot. Contrasting colors between the building and the signage help customers find you.
  • Context: Look at the buildings around you. You want to stand out without clashing. Complementary colors draw attention while maintaining neighborhood cohesion.
  • Local character: Many Oregon Coast towns have a distinct architectural character. Choose colors that respect the local aesthetic while expressing your brand.

Exterior Storefront Painting

The exterior of a retail building on the coast faces salt air, rain, wind, and UV exposure. These conditions demand high-quality exterior paints and proper preparation.

Surface Preparation

Professional storefront painting starts with thorough preparation. This includes pressure washing to remove dirt and grime, scraping any peeling paint, sanding rough surfaces, repairing any damaged trim or siding, and priming bare wood or previously unpainted surfaces. On the coast, checking for moisture damage and rot before painting is essential.

Product Selection

For coastal storefronts, use exterior acrylic latex paints rated for marine environments. These products resist salt air, moisture, and UV fading better than standard exterior paints. Semi-gloss or satin finishes on trim and doors provide durability and are easy to keep clean.

Interior Retail Painting

Your interior paint affects the shopping experience. Lighting, wall color, and finish all influence how your merchandise looks and how customers feel in the space.

  • Light colors make spaces feel larger and help merchandise stand out
  • Neutral tones serve as a backdrop that does not compete with products for attention
  • Accent walls can draw the eye toward featured displays or specific areas of the store
  • Durable finishes like eggshell or satin resist scuffs from foot traffic, shopping carts, and merchandise handling

Scheduling for Minimal Business Impact

Retail painting should be scheduled to minimize disruption. Options include:

  • Off-season work: If your business slows during the winter months, schedule interior and exterior work then.
  • Overnight or early morning painting: Interior work can often be completed before the store opens.
  • Section by section: For larger stores, paint one section at a time while the rest of the store operates normally.

Return on Investment

A fresh coat of paint is one of the most cost-effective improvements a retail business can make. It refreshes the customer experience, protects the building from weather damage, and can even increase foot traffic. Many property management companies and landlords require tenants to maintain their storefronts to a certain standard, making regular painting a lease obligation as well as a business strategy.
