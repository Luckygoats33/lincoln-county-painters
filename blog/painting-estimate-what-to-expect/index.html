What to Expect from a Painting Estimate

Lincoln County Painters

The Purpose of a Painting Estimate

A painting estimate is more than a number. It is a document that describes the scope of work, the materials to be used, the timeline, and the cost for your specific project. A thorough estimate gives you the information you need to make an informed decision and sets clear expectations for both you and the painting contractor.

Before the Estimate: What to Prepare

Before a painter visits your home, having a few things ready will help the estimate process go smoothly:

  • Know what you want painted: The entire exterior? Just the trim? Three interior rooms? The more specific you can be about the scope, the more accurate the estimate.
  • Note any problem areas: Point out peeling paint, water stains, soft spots in wood, or areas with mold or mildew growth. These affect preparation and cost.
  • Have color ideas: You do not need to have colors finalized, but knowing your general direction (neutrals, bold colors, matching a specific element) helps the painter plan.
  • Know your timeline: Are you flexible, or do you need the project completed by a specific date? This affects scheduling and potentially pricing.

The On-Site Visit

A reputable painter will visit your property in person before providing an estimate. Be wary of estimates given over the phone or based solely on photos, especially for exterior work where surface condition is critical.

During the visit, the painter should:

  • Walk the entire area to be painted and inspect all surfaces
  • Note the condition of existing paint, trim, siding, and substrates
  • Measure or estimate the square footage of paintable surfaces
  • Identify any repairs that need to happen before painting
  • Discuss access challenges, such as height, landscaping, or tight spaces
  • Ask about your preferences for paint products, colors, and finishes

What a Thorough Estimate Should Include

A professional painting estimate should provide clear details about every aspect of the project:

Scope of Work

A specific description of what will be painted. This should list the surfaces included: for example, all exterior siding, all window trim, fascia boards, front door, and garage door. Anything not listed should be confirmed as excluded.

Preparation Steps

The estimate should describe what preparation work will be performed: pressure washing, scraping, sanding, caulking, priming, patching, and any wood repair. Preparation is a major cost component, and understanding what is included prevents surprises.

Materials

The estimate should specify the paint brand, product line, finish type, and number of coats. This allows you to compare estimates fairly. An estimate that says "two coats of premium exterior paint" is more informative than one that just says "paint."

Timeline

When will the project start, and how long is it expected to take? Weather contingencies should be noted, especially for exterior work on the coast.

Cost

The total cost, with a breakdown if possible. Some painters provide line-item breakdowns; others provide a single project total. Both are legitimate, but a breakdown helps you understand where the money goes.

Payment Terms

When is payment due? Most reputable painters ask for a deposit to schedule the work, with the balance due upon completion and satisfaction.

Warranty

What does the painter warranty? For how long? What does the warranty cover, and what are the conditions?

Comparing Multiple Estimates

Getting two or three estimates is standard practice. When comparing, look beyond the bottom-line number:

  • Are all estimates covering the same scope of work?
  • Is one estimate skipping preparation steps that the others include?
  • Are the paint products comparable in quality?
  • Does each contractor carry insurance and hold an Oregon CCB license?

The lowest estimate is not automatically the best choice. It may indicate shortcuts in preparation, inferior products, or a lack of insurance. The best value is a fair price for quality work done right.
