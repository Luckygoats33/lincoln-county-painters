How to Choose the Right Exterior Paint Brand

Lincoln County Painters

Does the Paint Brand Really Matter?

Yes. While surface preparation and application technique are the most important factors in how long a paint job lasts, the paint product itself plays a significant role. Not all paints are formulated equally, and the differences become especially apparent in demanding environments like the Oregon Coast.

What Makes a Quality Exterior Paint

Resin Content

Resin (also called binder) is the ingredient that forms the actual paint film after the water or solvent evaporates. Higher resin content generally means better adhesion, flexibility, and durability. Premium paints have more resin and less filler than budget products. One hundred percent acrylic resin is the standard for quality exterior latex paints.

Pigment Quality

Titanium dioxide is the primary pigment used for hiding power — the ability of paint to cover the surface beneath it. Higher-quality paints use more titanium dioxide and less cheap filler like calcium carbonate. Better pigment means better coverage and more consistent color.

Additives

Quality exterior paints include additives for mildew resistance, UV protection, and flexibility. On the Oregon Coast, mildew resistance is particularly important. Paint without effective mildewcides will develop mildew growth within a year or two, regardless of how well it was applied.

Major Paint Brands Compared

The major paint manufacturers each offer products at several quality tiers. The brand name alone does not guarantee quality — what matters is which product line within that brand you choose.

Professional-Grade Lines

Each major manufacturer has a premium product line designed for professional use and demanding conditions. These products cost more per gallon but provide noticeably better coverage, adhesion, and longevity. They typically require fewer coats and hold their color longer.

Mid-Range Lines

Adequate for many applications, mid-range products are a reasonable choice when the surface is in good condition, preparation is thorough, and the environment is not exceptionally demanding. On the coast, however, the step up to a premium product is often worthwhile.

Budget Lines

Economy paints contain less resin, cheaper pigments, and fewer performance additives. They go on thinner, cover less well, and break down faster. For exterior use on the Oregon Coast, budget paint is generally not a good investment — you will likely need to repaint years sooner, spending more in total than if you had used a better product initially.

Why Your Painter's Recommendation Matters

Professional painters work with multiple products daily and see how they perform over time in real-world conditions. A painter who has worked extensively on the Oregon Coast knows which products hold up to salt air, moisture, and UV exposure — and which ones do not.

Ask your painter what product they plan to use and why. A quality contractor will have a clear rationale for their recommendation based on your specific surfaces, conditions, and budget. Be cautious of contractors who default to the cheapest available product without discussing alternatives.

Price Per Gallon vs. Cost Per Year

A premium paint might cost 50 to 70 percent more per gallon than a budget product. But if the premium paint lasts twice as long, it costs less per year of protection. When you factor in the labor cost of preparation and application — which is the same regardless of paint quality — the math strongly favors better products.
