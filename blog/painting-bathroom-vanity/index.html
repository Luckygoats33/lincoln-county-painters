Painting Your Bathroom Vanity: A Complete Guide

Lincoln County Painters

A Budget-Friendly Bathroom Upgrade

Replacing a bathroom vanity involves plumbing disconnection, countertop removal, and often wall repairs. Painting the existing vanity cabinet achieves a dramatic visual transformation at a fraction of the cost and disruption. It is one of the most impactful small updates you can make in a bathroom.

Can Your Vanity Be Painted?

Most bathroom vanities can be painted successfully. Solid wood, MDF, and plywood vanities all accept paint well with proper preparation. Laminate and thermofoil-wrapped vanities can also be painted but require specialized bonding primers and more careful preparation.

Vanities that are water-damaged, warped, or structurally failing are better candidates for replacement. If the cabinet carcass is sound and the doors close properly, painting is viable.

The Process

Step 1: Remove Hardware and Doors

Take off all handles, knobs, and hinges. Remove the doors and drawer fronts. Number each piece so they return to the correct position. If possible, remove the vanity doors from the bathroom to work on them in a clean, well-ventilated area.

Step 2: Clean Thoroughly

Bathroom vanities accumulate soap residue, toothpaste splatter, cosmetic products, and moisture-related buildup. Clean every surface with a degreasing cleaner and let it dry completely. Any residue left on the surface will prevent paint from adhering.

Step 3: Sand

Sand all surfaces with 150-grit sandpaper to remove gloss and create a surface profile for primer. On stained or varnished wood, sand until the sheen is gone. Wipe away all dust with a tack cloth.

Step 4: Prime

Apply a high-adhesion bonding primer to all surfaces. For bathroom use, a shellac-based or high-adhesion primer provides the best foundation — it blocks stains, seals the surface, and bonds tenaciously even to previously finished surfaces. Allow the primer to dry fully per the manufacturer's instructions.

Step 5: Light Sanding

Once primer is dry, sand lightly with 220-grit sandpaper to smooth any raised grain or texture. Remove dust with a tack cloth.

Step 6: Topcoat

Apply two coats of a durable cabinet-grade paint. Acrylic alkyd enamel or waterborne polyurethane enamel provides the moisture resistance and hardness that bathroom use demands. Apply with a high-quality brush, small foam roller, or spray equipment for the smoothest finish.

Step 7: Cure and Reinstall

Allow the final coat to cure for the time specified by the paint manufacturer — typically 3 to 7 days — before reinstalling hardware and doors. Rushing this step is the most common cause of damage to freshly painted cabinet surfaces.

Paint Selection for Bathrooms

Bathrooms are high-moisture environments. The paint you choose for a vanity needs to resist moisture, clean easily, and not yellow in the warm, humid conditions typical of bathrooms. Semi-gloss or satin sheens perform best because they shed moisture and resist staining.

Avoid standard wall paint on vanities. It is not formulated for the contact, cleaning, and moisture exposure that bathroom cabinets endure.

Color Ideas for Bathroom Vanities

  • Navy or deep blue: Creates a spa-like, sophisticated feel
  • Warm white or cream: Brightens the bathroom and makes it feel larger
  • Sage green: Calming and natural, pairs well with white fixtures
  • Charcoal or dark gray: Bold and contemporary with light countertops
