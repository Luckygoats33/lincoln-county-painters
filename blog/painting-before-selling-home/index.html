Painting Before Selling Your Home: What Is Worth It

Lincoln County Painters

Should You Paint Before Listing Your Home?

If you are preparing to sell your home in Lincoln County, painting is one of the most cost-effective improvements you can make. Fresh paint transforms the appearance of a home, makes spaces feel clean and move-in ready, and helps buyers focus on the property's strengths rather than its worn surfaces. The question is not whether to paint, but where to focus your budget for the greatest impact.

Exterior: The First Impression

Buyers form their opinion of your home within seconds of pulling into the driveway. This is why real estate professionals emphasize curb appeal above almost every other improvement.

When Exterior Painting Is Worth It

  • Peeling or flaking paint: This signals deferred maintenance and makes buyers worry about what else has been neglected.
  • Faded or dated colors: A home in a color that was popular twenty years ago can feel outdated before a buyer walks through the door.
  • Visible weather damage: On the Oregon Coast, salt air and moisture take a toll. Fresh exterior paint shows the home has been well maintained.

Smart Exterior Choices

  • Choose neutral, contemporary colors that appeal to the broadest range of buyers
  • Make sure the front door stands out — a freshly painted front door is one of the highest-ROI improvements you can make
  • Do not forget the trim, shutters, and garage door — these details complete the picture

Interior: Making Spaces Feel New

Inside the home, fresh paint makes rooms feel clean, bright, and well-cared-for. But you do not need to paint every room to get the benefit.

Highest-Impact Interior Areas

  • Kitchen: Buyers spend more time evaluating the kitchen than any other room. Fresh wall paint and possibly painted cabinets can transform a dated kitchen for far less than a remodel.
  • Bathrooms: Fresh, clean-looking bathrooms with bright paint and no mildew stains tell buyers the home is well maintained.
  • Living room and main entry: These are the first interior spaces buyers see. Make them count.
  • Master bedroom: After the kitchen and bathrooms, the master bedroom is where buyers focus.

Color Choices for Selling

When painting to sell, neutral colors are almost always the right choice:

  • Warm whites, light grays, and greiges (gray-beige blends) are universally appealing
  • Avoid bold or unusual colors that reflect personal taste
  • Consistent color throughout the home creates flow and makes spaces feel larger
  • White or off-white trim freshens the entire home

What You Can Skip

Not every surface needs painting before listing. If budget is limited, you can often skip:

  • Rooms with paint in good condition that is a neutral color
  • Closet interiors (unless they are in poor condition)
  • The garage (unless you are in a high-end market where every detail matters)
  • Exterior areas that are not visible from the street or main entrance

The Return on Investment

Painting is consistently cited as one of the highest-ROI home improvement projects. Real estate professionals routinely recommend it as a pre-listing investment because it costs relatively little compared to the perceived value increase. A freshly painted home photographs better for online listings, shows better in person, and can sell faster and for more than a comparable home with worn paint.

Timing Your Pre-Sale Painting

Plan to have painting completed before listing photos are taken. Ideally, finish painting at least a week before photography so the home is fully settled, furniture is back in place, and any paint odor has dissipated.
