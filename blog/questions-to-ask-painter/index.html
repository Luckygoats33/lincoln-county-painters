Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Painter

Lincoln County Painters

Ask the Right Questions, Get the Right Painter

Hiring a painting contractor is an investment in your home, and asking the right questions upfront helps you make an informed decision. These questions are not designed to grill potential contractors but to give you the information you need to compare options fairly and choose the best fit for your project.

Licensing and Insurance Questions

Are you licensed with the Oregon CCB?

This is the most fundamental question. Oregon law requires painters to hold an active Construction Contractors Board license. Ask for the license number and verify it online. An unlicensed contractor leaves you without consumer protections if something goes wrong.

Do you carry general liability insurance and workers' compensation?

General liability insurance protects your property if the contractor causes damage. Workers' compensation protects you from liability if a worker is injured on your property. Ask to see certificates of insurance, and verify coverage is current. A legitimate contractor will provide this information without hesitation.

Experience and References Questions

How long have you been painting professionally?

Experience matters, especially on the Oregon Coast where conditions demand specialized knowledge. A contractor with years of experience in coastal environments has encountered and solved the challenges your project is likely to present.

Do you have experience with my type of project?

If you need cabinet painting, ask specifically about cabinet experience. If you have a historic home, ask about lead paint experience. If you need commercial painting, ask about scheduling around business operations. Not all painters have experience with every type of project.

Can you provide references from recent local projects?

References from projects in Lincoln County give you the most relevant information. Ask for references from projects similar to yours in scope and type.

Project-Specific Questions

What preparation will you do before painting?

This question reveals a lot about a contractor's approach to quality. A thorough answer should include washing, scraping, sanding, patching, caulking, and priming as appropriate for your surfaces. Vague answers like "we will prep as needed" should prompt follow-up questions.

What products will you use?

Your contractor should be able to tell you the specific brand, product line, and sheen for both primer and topcoat. On the coast, products rated for marine or high-humidity environments perform significantly better. Ask why they chose those products and what alternatives exist.

How many coats will you apply?

The industry standard for a quality paint job is one coat of primer on bare or problem surfaces and two coats of topcoat. If a contractor plans to apply only one topcoat, ask why and understand that it may affect the longevity and appearance of the finish.

What is included in the estimate?

Make sure you understand exactly what is covered. Are small repairs included? Caulking? Moving furniture? Protecting landscaping? The more detailed the estimate, the fewer surprises you will have during the project.

Timeline and Logistics Questions

When can you start, and how long will it take?

Get specific start and completion dates. Understand what happens if weather delays the project, especially for exterior work on the coast.

How many crew members will be on site?

This affects both the timeline and the level of supervision. A single painter handles everything personally. A larger crew requires good supervision to maintain quality and consistency.

How do you handle weather delays?

This is particularly important on the Oregon Coast. A professional contractor should have a clear plan for weather interruptions, including how they protect work in progress and how delays affect the schedule.

Warranty and Payment Questions

What warranty do you offer?

Understand what the warranty covers, how long it lasts, and what would void it. A warranty should cover defects in workmanship, meaning problems caused by improper application or preparation, not normal wear or damage from external causes.

What is your payment schedule?

Be cautious of contractors who demand full payment upfront. A typical arrangement is a deposit to secure the scheduling, a progress payment midway through larger projects, and a final payment upon satisfactory completion. Oregon law limits contractor deposits to specific amounts.

The Decision

The right answers to these questions paint a picture of a professional, experienced contractor who takes your project seriously. Compare the responses from multiple contractors, weigh them against your priorities, and choose the contractor who gives you the most confidence in a quality result.
