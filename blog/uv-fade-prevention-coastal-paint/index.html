Preventing UV Fade on Coastal Home Paint

Lincoln County Painters

UV Exposure on the Oregon Coast

People often assume that because the Oregon Coast is known for clouds and fog, UV damage is not a concern. This is a misconception. UV rays penetrate cloud cover, and the reflective qualities of water and sand can actually intensify UV exposure on surfaces near the shore. Homes in Depoe Bay, Yachats, and other oceanfront communities often show significant UV fading on their south and west-facing walls.

How UV Light Damages Paint

Ultraviolet light breaks down the chemical bonds in paint resins and pigments. Over time, this causes:

  • Color fading — Pigments lose their vibrancy and depth, leaving a washed-out appearance
  • Chalking — The paint surface deteriorates into a powdery residue that wipes off on your hand
  • Loss of sheen — Glossy and satin finishes become flat and dull
  • Reduced protection — As the paint film degrades, it provides less protection against moisture

On the coast, UV damage often works in combination with salt and moisture. Salt crystals on the surface can act as tiny magnifiers, concentrating UV energy on the paint beneath them.

Choosing UV-Resistant Paint

Quality Pigments

Higher-quality paints use better pigments that resist UV degradation. Inorganic pigments, like those found in earth tones and whites, tend to hold up better than some organic pigments used in brighter colors. This does not mean you cannot use bold colors on the coast, but it does mean investing in a paint that uses UV-stable pigment technology.

UV-Resistant Resins

The resin system in the paint is what holds everything together. Premium exterior paints use acrylic resins formulated with UV stabilizers and absorbers. These additives help the resin resist breakdown from UV exposure. Cheaper paints use lower-quality resins that degrade faster.

Light-Reflective Formulations

Some exterior paints are formulated to reflect UV light rather than absorb it. These can be particularly effective on surfaces that receive intense sun exposure. Ask your painter about products designed for high-UV environments.

Color Choices That Last

Some colors hold up better than others against UV exposure:

  • Earth tones — Beiges, tans, and warm grays use inorganic pigments that resist fading well
  • Whites and off-whites — Titanium dioxide, the primary white pigment, is naturally UV resistant
  • Deep blues and greens — These fade more noticeably than earth tones but quality formulations hold up reasonably well
  • Reds and yellows — Traditional formulations of these colors are most susceptible to fading, though modern pigment technology has improved their longevity

If you love a color that is known to fade, choose it from a premium paint line and apply an extra coat on the most sun-exposed surfaces. The additional film thickness provides more pigment to protect against UV breakdown.

Additional Protection Strategies

Beyond paint selection, you can reduce UV damage through:

  • Landscaping — Trees and shrubs that provide afternoon shade on south and west walls reduce UV exposure without blocking views
  • Extended eaves and overhangs — These shade upper portions of walls from direct sun
  • Regular washing — Removing salt deposits prevents them from intensifying UV damage on the paint surface

When to Repaint

If your paint is visibly faded or chalking, it is time to consider repainting. Running your hand across the surface and finding a powdery residue on your palm is a clear sign. Exterior painting with a UV-resistant product restores both the appearance and protective function of your home's finish.
