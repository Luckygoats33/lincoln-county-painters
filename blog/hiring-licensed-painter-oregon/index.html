Why You Should Hire a Licensed Painter in Oregon

Lincoln County Painters

Oregon Contractor Licensing Exists to Protect You

Oregon has some of the strongest contractor licensing laws in the country, and they exist for one reason: to protect consumers. The Construction Contractors Board (CCB) regulates all contractors in Oregon, including painting contractors. Understanding what a CCB license means and why it matters helps you make a safer hiring decision.

What a CCB License Requires

To obtain and maintain an Oregon CCB license, a painting contractor must:

  • Pass a business law examination demonstrating knowledge of Oregon construction law, lien laws, and consumer protection regulations
  • Carry general liability insurance with minimum coverage amounts set by the state, protecting your property if the contractor causes damage
  • Maintain a surety bond that provides a financial guarantee of performance. If the contractor fails to complete work or does defective work, the bond provides funds for compensation
  • Register their business with the state and maintain compliance with Oregon business requirements
  • Complete continuing education to keep their license current

Consumer Protections You Get with a Licensed Contractor

CCB Complaint Process

If you have a dispute with a licensed contractor, you can file a complaint with the CCB. The board investigates complaints and has the authority to mediate disputes, order corrective work, impose fines, and even revoke licenses. This process gives consumers a structured way to resolve problems without going directly to court.

With an unlicensed contractor, this process is not available to you. Your only recourse is civil court, which is more expensive, more time-consuming, and less likely to result in a satisfactory resolution.

Surety Bond Claims

If a licensed contractor causes financial harm through defective work, failure to complete work, or other covered situations, you can file a claim against their surety bond. This provides a source of compensation even if the contractor is unable or unwilling to resolve the issue directly.

Insurance Protection

If a licensed contractor damages your property, their general liability insurance covers the cost of repairs. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor carries workers' compensation insurance (required for contractors with employees), you are protected from liability for those medical costs.

The Risks of Hiring Unlicensed

Hiring an unlicensed painter may seem like a way to save money, but the risks are significant:

No Insurance Protection

If an unlicensed painter damages your property, breaks a window, or spills paint on your flooring, you have no insurance to cover the loss. You would need to pursue the individual in court, and collecting a judgment against an uninsured individual can be nearly impossible.

Worker Injury Liability

If an unlicensed, uninsured worker falls off a ladder on your property, you could be held liable for their medical expenses and lost wages. This is a real risk, and the costs can be devastating. Homeowner's insurance policies may not cover injuries to workers hired without proper insurance.

No Recourse for Bad Work

Without the CCB complaint process and surety bond, your options for addressing poor workmanship are limited to civil court. Many homeowners find that the cost of litigation exceeds the cost of the original paint job.

Potential Legal Issues for You

Oregon law makes it illegal for unlicensed contractors to perform work that requires a license. While the penalties fall primarily on the contractor, knowingly hiring an unlicensed contractor can complicate insurance claims and legal proceedings if problems arise.

How to Verify a License

Verifying a contractor's CCB license takes about two minutes:

  1. Ask the contractor for their CCB license number
  2. Visit the CCB website at oregon.gov/ccb
  3. Search for the contractor by name or license number
  4. Verify that the license is active and not suspended or expired
  5. Check for any complaints or disciplinary actions on record

A reputable contractor will provide their CCB number without hesitation. It should appear on their business card, website, contract, and estimate.

Beyond the License

A CCB license is the minimum baseline, not a guarantee of quality. It tells you the contractor meets the legal requirements to operate in Oregon. Beyond the license, evaluate contractors based on experience, references, the quality of their estimates, and their knowledge of coastal painting requirements.
