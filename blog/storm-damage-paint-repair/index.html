Repairing Storm-Damaged Paint on Coastal Homes

Lincoln County Painters

After the Storm: Assessing the Damage

Every winter, storms batter the Central Oregon Coast with wind, rain, and salt spray. When the skies clear, homeowners in Newport, Depoe Bay, Lincoln City, and throughout Lincoln County often find their exterior paint has taken a beating. Prompt assessment and repair prevent minor damage from turning into major problems.

Common Storm Damage to Paint

Wind-Blown Debris Impacts

Branches, gravel, and other debris carried by coastal winds can chip paint on impact. These chips expose bare substrate to moisture, creating entry points for water damage. Even small chips should be addressed before the next rain.

Peeling from Water Infiltration

Wind-driven rain forces water behind siding and into joints that would normally stay dry. When this water dries, it can push paint off from underneath, creating bubbles and peeling sections. This type of damage often appears a few days or weeks after a storm, once the trapped moisture begins to dry.

Salt Encrustation

Major storms deposit heavy layers of salt on exterior surfaces. If this salt is not washed off, it continues to attract moisture and accelerates paint degradation. A thorough rinse with fresh water after major storms removes salt before it causes lasting damage.

Gutter and Downspout Damage

When storms damage gutters or downspouts, water cascades directly onto siding and trim. This concentrated water flow quickly overwhelms paint and caulk, causing localized damage. Repairing drainage issues promptly protects the paint and the structure beneath it.

Step-by-Step Repair Process

Step 1: Inspect the Entire Exterior

Walk around your home and look at every surface. Check each wall, paying particular attention to the storm-facing sides. Note any chipped, peeling, or blistered paint. Look for water stains below windows and at joints. Check that all caulk is intact. Test any questionable wood by pressing on it with a screwdriver to check for softness.

Step 2: Clean the Surfaces

Before any repair, wash the damaged areas thoroughly. A pressure washer on a gentle setting removes salt, dirt, and loose paint. Let everything dry completely before proceeding. On the coast, this may require waiting for a dry spell.

Step 3: Remove Damaged Paint

Scrape and sand any peeling, flaking, or blistered paint back to a sound surface. Feather the edges of surrounding paint so the transition is smooth. Collect and dispose of paint chips, especially on older homes where lead paint may be present.

Step 4: Repair the Substrate

If the wood beneath the paint is damaged, it needs to be repaired or replaced before repainting. Small areas of deterioration can be treated with wood hardener and filled with exterior wood filler. Larger areas of rot require board replacement.

Step 5: Prime and Paint

Apply a quality exterior primer to all bare surfaces. Once the primer is dry, apply topcoat to match the existing finish. For spot repairs, try to blend the new paint into the surrounding area. Full wall repaints sometimes look better than patchwork, especially when the existing paint has faded.

When Spot Repair Is Enough

If damage is limited to a few chips and small sections, spot repair is often sufficient. Touch up the affected areas, recaulk any failed joints, and monitor the rest of the exterior through the season.

When a Full Repaint Is Needed

If storm damage reveals widespread underlying problems such as extensive peeling across multiple walls, significant wood deterioration, or an overall paint film that has reached the end of its life, a complete exterior repaint is the better investment. Patching a failing overall finish is a losing battle.

Preventing Future Damage

While you cannot control the weather, you can prepare for it. Maintain your gutters and drainage systems. Keep trees trimmed back from the house. Ensure caulk and flashing are in good condition before storm season. And make sure your exterior paint is in good shape going into fall.
