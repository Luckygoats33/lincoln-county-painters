How Long Does Cabinet Painting Take?

Lincoln County Painters

Planning Around Cabinet Painting

One of the first questions homeowners ask about cabinet painting is how long the process takes. Understanding the timeline helps you plan meals, manage daily routines, and set realistic expectations for when your kitchen will be back to normal.

Typical Timeline

A professional cabinet painting project for a standard-sized kitchen typically takes five to ten working days from start to finish. This breaks down roughly as follows:

  • Day 1: Removal of doors, drawer fronts, and hardware. Labeling and documentation. Kitchen protection setup.
  • Days 2-3: Cleaning, sanding, filling, and preparation of all surfaces — both the removed pieces and the cabinet boxes that remain on the wall.
  • Day 4: Priming all surfaces.
  • Day 5: Light sanding of primer, first topcoat application.
  • Day 6: Light sanding between coats, second topcoat application.
  • Days 7-8: Curing time — the paint needs adequate time to harden before doors are handled and reinstalled.
  • Day 9-10: Reinstallation of doors, drawer fronts, and hardware. Final adjustments and cleanup.

What Affects the Timeline

Kitchen Size

A small galley kitchen with a dozen doors and a few drawers takes less time than a large kitchen with 30 or more doors, multiple drawer banks, and a pantry cabinet. The number of individual pieces to prepare, prime, and paint is the primary driver of project length.

Cabinet Condition

Cabinets with heavy grease buildup, damaged surfaces, deep grain that needs filling (common with oak cabinets), or peeling finish require more preparation time. Clean, smooth cabinets in good condition move through preparation faster.

Color Change

Changing from a dark stain to a light painted finish — or any dramatic color change — may require an additional coat of primer or an extra topcoat for complete coverage. Each additional coat adds at least one day for application and drying.

Complexity

Crown molding on upper cabinets, glass-panel doors, decorative details, and island cabinets with exposed sides all add complexity and time to the project.

Living Without Cabinet Doors

During most of the project, your cabinet boxes remain on the wall — accessible and functional, just without doors. You can still use your dishes, cookware, and supplies. The sink, stove, and refrigerator remain usable throughout.

Many homeowners find this less disruptive than expected. The kitchen feels open and casual, and the inconvenience is temporary.

Some tips for managing the process:

  • Clear items from the countertops closest to the cabinet boxes being painted
  • Plan simpler meals or use a temporary cooking setup for the days when the kitchen is most disrupted
  • Keep frequently used items accessible and move less-used items to another room temporarily

Why Rushing the Timeline Is a Mistake

Each step in the cabinet painting process requires proper drying and curing time. Applying the next coat before the previous one has dried results in adhesion failures, wrinkling, and a finish that remains soft and prone to damage. Reinstalling doors before the paint has fully cured leads to sticking, peeling, and fingerprint marks in the finish.

Quality cabinet painting cannot be rushed. The timeline exists because each step depends on the previous one being complete.
