Exterior Paint Color Trends for 2026

Lincoln County Painters

What Is Trending in Exterior Color

Exterior paint color choices tend to evolve more slowly than interior trends because homeowners live with their exterior color for years. That said, clear patterns emerge each year as paint manufacturers release their color forecasts and real-world projects reveal what homeowners are actually choosing.

For 2026, the trends lean toward colors that connect homes to their natural surroundings — something that resonates particularly well on the Oregon Coast.

Nature-Inspired Tones

Warm Greens

Sage, olive, and muted forest greens continue to grow in popularity. These colors blend beautifully with the evergreen-heavy landscape of the Oregon Coast, creating homes that look rooted in their environment rather than imposed on it. Warm greens work especially well on craftsman-style and cottage-style homes common in Yachats and Waldport.

Earthy Blues

Not bright or nautical, but deep, muted blues that recall the ocean on an overcast day. Think slate blue, stormy blue, and blue-gray tones. These pair well with white or cream trim and work on almost any architectural style.

Warm Neutrals

Greige (gray-beige), warm taupe, and clay tones provide a sophisticated neutral that reads as more interesting than plain gray or beige. These colors photograph well, appeal broadly for resale, and complement natural wood accents like cedar shingle details or exposed beams.

Classic Coastal Palettes

While trends come and go, certain color combinations have proven timeless on the coast:

  • Weathered gray body with white trim: Evokes driftwood and works with the natural graying of untreated wood around it
  • Deep navy body with bright white trim: Classic and striking, especially on homes with good architectural detail
  • Warm white or cream overall: Clean, bright, and reflective — helps homes stand out against the green landscape
  • Natural wood stain with painted trim: Preserves the character of cedar or other wood siding while defining edges and details with color

Colors to Approach Carefully

Some colors that look appealing in photos or on color chips present practical challenges on the coast:

  • Very dark colors: Absorb more heat and UV radiation, which accelerates fading and can cause wood to warp. They also show every speck of dust and salt residue.
  • Bright whites: Beautiful when freshly painted but show dirt, mildew, and discoloration faster than off-whites or warm whites in the damp coastal environment.
  • Trendy or unusual colors: A bold color choice that looks great today may feel dated in a few years and can affect resale value.

How to Choose Your Color

The best way to test an exterior color is with large paint samples applied directly to your siding. View them at different times of day and in different weather conditions — the overcast light common on the Oregon Coast makes colors appear quite different than they would in bright inland sunshine.

Consider your fixed elements: roof color, stone or brick accents, landscaping, and neighboring homes. Your paint color should complement these rather than clash with them.
