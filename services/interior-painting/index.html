Interior Painting

Interior Painting for Coastal Oregon Homes

Every room, every surface, every detail. Thorough preparation, clean application, and finishes chosen to perform in coastal conditions.

What We Paint

Every Surface Inside Your Home

Our interior painting service covers the full scope of what most homeowners need. We do not specialize in one surface and subcontract the rest. When we take on an interior project, we handle all of it.

  • Walls — Living rooms, bedrooms, hallways, stairwells, bathrooms, and kitchens. Single accent walls or full-room repaints.
  • Ceilings — Flat ceilings, vaulted ceilings, and popcorn/textured ceiling surfaces. We address stains, water marks, and discoloration.
  • Trim & Baseboards — Crown molding, window casings, door frames, and baseboards. We sand, prime, and apply smooth finishes.
  • Doors — Interior doors painted on-site or removed for a smoother finish. Closet doors, pantry doors, and bi-folds included.
  • Closets & Utility Rooms — Often overlooked, always included when they are within the project scope.
Our Process

Preparation Is Most of the Work

The difference between a paint job that looks good on day one and one that still looks good in three years is almost entirely about what happens before the roller touches the wall. We spend more time on preparation than most homeowners expect, and that is by design.

Furniture and floor protection comes first. We move furniture to the center of the room or out entirely, cover everything with clean drop cloths, and mask off fixtures, hardware, and surfaces that are not being painted. Carpet edges, hardwood floors, and countertops are all covered and taped.

Surface preparation follows. We fill nail holes, patch drywall cracks, caulk gaps between trim and walls, and sand rough spots smooth. If there is peeling or bubbling paint, we scrape it back to a stable edge. Glossy surfaces get scuff-sanded so primer and paint can bond properly.

Priming is not optional. New drywall, repaired patches, stain-prone areas, and surfaces changing from dark to light all receive dedicated primer before the finish coats go on. On bare wood trim, we use a bonding primer that seals the grain and prevents tannin bleed-through.

Only after all of this is complete do we apply the finish coats — typically two coats on walls, two coats on trim, with drying time between each.

Paint Selection

Choosing the Right Paint for Coastal Interiors

Living near the ocean changes how interior paint performs. Higher humidity levels, salt-laden air that finds its way inside, and condensation on cool surfaces all play a role. We help you choose products that account for these conditions.

Sheen Levels Matter

We recommend specific sheen levels based on room use. Flat or matte for ceilings and low-traffic rooms where you want minimal light reflection. Eggshell or satin for living areas and bedrooms — easy to clean without looking shiny. Semi-gloss for trim, doors, bathrooms, and kitchens where moisture resistance and washability are priorities.

Moisture-Resistant Formulas

For bathrooms, kitchens, and laundry rooms in coastal homes, we use paints formulated with mildew-resistant additives. These products are designed to resist the growth of mold and mildew on the paint film, which is especially relevant when indoor humidity stays elevated through the wet season — roughly October through May on the Oregon Coast.

Color Consultation

We can help you narrow down colors and test them in your actual lighting before committing. Coastal light is different from inland light — the gray skies, north-facing windows, and reflected ocean light all shift how colors read on your walls. We bring large sample swatches and paint test patches so you can see the actual result.

Coastal Conditions

Why Interior Painting on the Coast Is Different

Lincoln County averages roughly 70 inches of rain per year. That moisture does not stay outside. It finds its way into walls, crawl spaces, and living areas. Homes closer to the ocean also deal with salt-laden air that accelerates corrosion on surfaces and hardware. Adequate ventilation, proper primer, and moisture-tolerant finishes are not upgrades here — they are baseline requirements for interior paint that lasts.

Timeline

How Long Does Interior Painting Take?

Every project is different. These are general ranges based on scope. The actual timeline depends on surface condition, the amount of prep work needed, number of colors, and drying conditions.

1 – 2 Days

Single Room

One bedroom or living room. Walls, ceiling, and trim. Assumes surfaces are in fair condition and do not require extensive repair.

3 – 5 Days

Several Rooms

Three to five rooms — for example, a main living area, kitchen, and two bedrooms. More prep time, more coats, more detail work on trim.

5 – 10 Days

Full Interior

Whole-home repaint including hallways, closets, and all trim. Larger homes, older homes with more prep needs, or homes with high ceilings may take longer.
