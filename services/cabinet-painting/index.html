Cabinet Painting & Refinishing

Transform Your Kitchen Without Tearing It Apart

Professionally sprayed cabinet finishes that look and feel like factory — at a fraction of the cost of replacing your cabinetry.

Kitchen Transformation

New Cabinets Cost a Fortune. Paint Changes Everything.

Cabinets dominate the visual space of any kitchen. When they look dated — whether it is honey oak from the 1990s, dark cherry that makes the room feel small, or just worn-out finishes showing every year of use — the whole kitchen feels tired. But ripping out cabinets and replacing them is one of the most expensive home renovations you can undertake.

Cabinet painting gives you a completely different kitchen for a fraction of the cost. The boxes stay. The layout stays. What changes is the surface: old stain or outdated color gets replaced with a smooth, sprayed finish in whatever color you choose. The result is cabinets that look and feel like new — because the finish is new.

This is not a DIY brush-and-roller job. The difference between professional spray-applied cabinet paint and a homeowner-applied finish is immediately visible and tangible. We spray because that is how cabinets are finished in the factory, and that is the standard your eye expects.

Our Process

Seven Steps to a Factory-Quality Finish

Cabinet painting is methodical work. Skipping steps shows in the final product. Here is exactly what we do, in order.

Remove & Label

Every door, drawer front, and piece of hardware is removed and labeled. Hinges, pulls, and knobs are bagged and tagged so everything goes back exactly where it came from.

Clean & Degrease

Kitchen cabinets accumulate years of cooking grease, especially above the stove. We thoroughly clean all surfaces with a degreaser. Paint will not adhere to grease, no matter how well you sand.

Sand & Degloss

Existing finishes are sanded to create tooth for the new coating. For polyurethane-sealed cabinets, we degloss the surface chemically and mechanically. Every surface that will receive paint gets sanded.

Fill & Repair

Dings, dents, old hardware holes, and imperfections are filled with appropriate filler and sanded smooth. The goal is a surface that looks new, not painted over.

Prime

A bonding primer designed for cabinetry is applied. This is critical — it is the layer that grabs onto the old surface and provides a foundation for the topcoat. We use primer specifically formulated for the substrate (wood, MDF, or laminate).

Spray Topcoat

Two coats of cabinet-grade paint are sprayed using HVLP or airless equipment. Spraying delivers an even, smooth coat without brush marks or roller stipple. Doors and drawer fronts are sprayed off-site or in a controlled area of your home.

Reinstall

Once fully cured, all doors, drawers, and hardware are reinstalled. Hinges are adjusted so doors hang level and close properly. We leave your kitchen ready to use.

Spray vs. Brush

Why Spray Finishing Matters

You might wonder why we do not just brush or roll cabinet paint. The answer is visible the moment you run your hand across a sprayed door versus a brushed one.

A brushed cabinet finish has texture — you can see and feel the brush strokes. Light catches them at angles, and they collect dust and grime in the tiny grooves. Over time, brushed cabinets look progressively more homemade as those textures become more pronounced.

A sprayed finish is glass-smooth. There are no directional marks, no stipple, no variation. It is the same finish you would find on brand-new cabinets from any quality manufacturer. That smoothness is not just cosmetic — it also cleans more easily because there are no grooves for kitchen grease and dust to settle into.

Spraying does require more preparation (masking, setting up spray zones) and more skill. But the result is worth it. If you are going to paint your cabinets, doing it right means spraying them.

Cabinet Materials

What Cabinets Can (and Cannot) Be Painted

Not all cabinets are created equal, and the material affects how we approach the job:

  • Solid wood — The ideal candidate. Whether oak, maple, cherry, or birch, solid wood cabinets accept paint beautifully with proper prep. Wood grain may telegraph slightly through paint, which is normal and expected on raised-panel doors.
  • MDF (medium-density fiberboard) — Excellent for painting. MDF is actually smoother than wood and produces a very uniform finish. Many new cabinets use MDF doors specifically because of how well they paint.
  • Plywood — Takes paint well with appropriate primer. Often used for cabinet boxes even when doors are solid wood.
  • Laminate and thermofoil — These can be painted, but require specific bonding primers and the results may not be as durable as painting wood or MDF. We will be upfront about expectations if your cabinets are laminate. In some cases, painting is the right move; in others, replacement makes more sense.
  • Metal cabinets — Older metal cabinets (common in mid-century homes) can be painted with metal-specific coatings. These are less common on the coast but we do encounter them in Toledo and other inland communities.
Planning Your Project

Timeline, Color, and Hardware

What to expect when you commit to a cabinet painting project.

Typical Timeline: 5-7 Days

An average kitchen with 20 to 30 doors and drawer fronts takes about five to seven working days. Day one is removal and prep. Days two through four are priming and spraying. Days five through seven are final coats, curing, and reinstallation. Your kitchen remains usable throughout — you just will not have doors on some cabinets for a few days.

Coastal Kitchen Colors

White and off-white remain the most popular cabinet colors, and for good reason — they brighten kitchens that may not get a lot of direct sunlight, especially on the coast. Soft grays, sage greens, and deep navy are also popular choices that work well with the coastal palette. We bring sample chips and can spray test panels so you see the color on your actual cabinet material before committing.

Hardware Refresh

While cabinet doors are off, it is the perfect time to update hardware. New pulls and knobs can further modernize the look. If your existing hardware is in good shape and you like it, we clean it and reinstall. If you want new hardware that uses different hole spacing, we fill the old holes during the repair step and drill new ones to match.
